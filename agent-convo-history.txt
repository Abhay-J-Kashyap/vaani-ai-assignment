> right now you're grading it, but what can be used as grading in production??

● In production there's no answer key, and nobody grades every answer. Quality
  is tracked through a few layers, from cheapest to most expensive. The first
  already exists in our pipeline.

  1. Free signals on every request (already in our trace)
  - Gate withholding rate: the share of drafted sentences the citation gate
    blocked. A rising rate means the drafter is making claims the passages don't
    support. For example, 6 of the 18 shorter 20b answers in the
    daily-exhausted run had a sentence withheld.
  - Refusal rate, split by retrieval confidence: a refusal when retrieval
    confidence was high is suspicious, because the papers probably did cover the
    question.
  - Tier mix and truncation: how often people got a 20b answer, quoted passages,
    or an answer cut off.

  These cost nothing, but they're proxies. They catch unsupported claims, not a
  correct number taken from the wrong table row.

  2. What the user does
  - Thumbs up or down on an answer.
  - Rephrasing the same question within a minute, which is a strong sign the
    answer missed.
  - Copying the answer or clicking a citation, which are weak signs it helped.

  3. A model grading a small sample, offline
  - Grade 2–5% of answers without an answer key:
    - Does each claim follow from the passage it cites? This is the semantic
      version of our gate.
    - Does the answer address the question?
    - Was a refusal justified by what was retrieved?
  - Run it after hours on daily budget that would otherwise go unused. The
    budget refills continuously, so anything unspent by the evening is free.
    This is the per-request Groq judge you rejected, but as a small sample it
    fits the budget.

  4. The gold set as a regression check
  - Re-run your 20 questions against the frozen key whenever the prompt, model
    or retrieval setting changes, and weekly to catch Groq changing the model
    underneath us.
  - That's the only place the four grades (correct / wrong / refused_correctly /
    refused_wrongly) exist in production.
  - Each run costs about 60K tokens, 30% of a day, so run it at night or on a
    subset.

  5. People review the risky cases
  - A person reads a targeted sample, not a random one: answers where the gate
    withheld something, high-confidence refusals, shorter 20b answers, and
    answers users rephrased.

  Something we can do now for no tokens: check whether the layer 1 signals
  actually predict the 80 grades we already have. For example, are answers with
  a withheld sentence or low retrieval confidence more often wrong? If they are,
  we can say in the write-up that we know which free signal to watch in
